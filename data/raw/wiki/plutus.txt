Plutus was the Greek God of Greed. He held auctions of various rare and precious artifacts. He was betrayed and killed by his assistant Beau.

[СИЛЫ И СПОСОБНОСТИ]
As a god, Plutus possesses all the standard powers of one, however he wasn't able to display many of his powers before he was vanquished. 
Spell Casting - A very powerful caster, he was able to hex his building to protect himself from Heaven, Hell, monsters, and other gods.
Super Strength - Since Plutus is a god, he possesses super strength, however he was destroyed before he was able to display this power.
Immortality - As a Deity, he could live forever. He has been alive since at least Ancient Greece. While he appeared as an older, almost elderly man, this did not at all affect his abilities.
Invulnerability - Being a god, he couldn't be killed by mortal weapons or ailments. However he was killed by an unknown special weaponized stake by his assistant …

[СЛАБОСТИ]
Wooden Stakes - He was killed by being stabbed with a stake by his assistant Beau.

[ИСТОРИЯ]
===Season 8===
Sometime after losing his worshipers, he set up in Wyoming an auction, guarded by other, lesser pagan deities, that dealt in rare and exclusive items such as the Mjölnir and the Amulet of Hesperus, which he auctioned to his clientele. After a thief stole the Word of God, he was able to get hold of it and auctioned it.

After a bidding match between Crowley and Samandriel, he took Kevin Tran, and added him to the item, Kevin's mother sold her soul to him in exchange for both. Although Crowley offered more, he took Mrs. Tran's offer because it was worth far more to her than the souls were to Crowley. When Crowley tried to bargain with his own soul, Plutus laughed and taunted Crowley, saying the demon didn't have a soul.

[ЛОР]
Ploutos, usually Romanized as Plutus, was the god of wealth in ancient Greek religion and myth. His mother is the goddess Demeter which makes him a nephew of Zeus.

He was often confused with Pluto, the Roman god of Underworld. Dean actually makes this mistake, upon first hearing his name. Pluto is actually the Roman counterpart of Hades.

In art Plutus was usually depicted as a boy holding a cornucopia filled with grain in the company of his mother Demeter.
